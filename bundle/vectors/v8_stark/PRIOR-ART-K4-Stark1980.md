## In plain words

**Claim 1 (2/f(sqrt -p)^2 is the rank-one Stark unit for S = {inf, P, P-bar}): TAKEN in substance by Stark 1980.** Stark proves his conjecture for every imaginary quadratic field (Theorem 2, p. 199). In the proof (pp. 226-227) he works out the case where the conductor is f = (2) "which may or may not be a prime ideal". His L-series drop the Euler factors at every prime dividing f (p. 198). So when 2 splits as P P-bar, f = (2) is exactly S = {inf, P, P-bar}. For that case he writes the Stark number down explicitly as an eta quotient, [2^{1/2} eta(2 theta)/eta(theta)]^2 with theta = omega = (1 + sqrt(-p))/2. On p. 218 he also says his eta quotient f(omega) "is equal to the Schlafli f-function at (-Delta)^{1/2}", which is Weber's f(sqrt -p). Put the two sentences together and you get our unit, up to a root of unity.

What Stark does NOT print: the words "p = 7 mod 8", the symbol f(sqrt -p) inside the Stark-unit statement, the final unit 2/f(sqrt -p)^2 written out, and the closing remark "this improves everything by a factor of 2" turned into the formula epsilon = phi(0, 1/2, omega). Those steps are ours. Each is one line of bookkeeping (below). So we should cite Stark 1980, pp. 226-227 (with p. 218), as the source of Claim 1, not present it as new.

**Claim 2 (Ramanujan's b_1 splits into three conductor-q Siegel/Robert units times a Delta-quotient, and b_1 is not a Stark unit for any S): OPEN as far as Stark 1980 goes.** Stark has no Ramanujan, no Ramanujan-type theta quotient f(a, b), no non-Stark unit, and no decomposition of such a unit into elliptic units. He does derive both Kronecker limit formulas side by side (first: eq. (8), p. 206; second: eq. (11), p. 207). He also has the Delta quotient Delta(Bz)/Delta(z) with its ideal factorization (Lemma 2, p. 212), but he uses it only to prove the reciprocity law, never in an L-value identity. The nearest thing to a "combination of Stark numbers" is the Coates-Wiles example on p. 229, where quotients of the pi(c) are units. That is a quotient of Stark numbers, not our split.

**Citable theorem for the imaginary quadratic rank-one case:** Theorem 2, p. 199 ("Conjecture 1 is true for k = Q and for complex quadratic k"). It is proved in Section 5, "Proof of Theorem 2 for complex quadratic fields", pp. 222-227, using Lemma 7 (pp. 222-223, from the second limit formula (11), p. 207), Lemma 8 (p. 224) and Lemma 9 (p. 225). The 2-part cases f = p_2 and f = (2) are on pp. 226-227. A worked example is on p. 228 (k = Q(sqrt -15), f = p_2).

## Derivation glue for Claim 1 (OUR steps, not Stark's text)

1. If p = 7 mod 8 then 2 = P P-bar in k = Q(sqrt -p). Then (O/2O)^x is trivial, so the ray class group mod (2) is the class group and K(2) = H. Every ray class character mod (2) is a class-group character with chi(P) = chi(P-bar) = 0. So Stark's L(s, chi) is L(s, chi)(1 - chi(P)2^{-s})(1 - chi(P-bar)2^{-s}), as the p. 198 sentence says.
2. w(f) = 2, since -1 = 1 mod (2). W(H) = 2 for p >= 7 prime: H/k is unramified, but k(i) and k(sqrt -3) ramify over k. Lemma 7 (p. 223) gives z'(0, c) = -(2/w(f)) log|phi(u, v, theta)| = -log|phi(0, 1/2, theta_c)|. This matches Conjecture 1(i) with W = 2 and eps(c) = phi(0, 1/2, theta_c) up to a root of unity. That is what "improves everything by a factor of 2" means; Stark does not print the formula for eps.
3. With b = [omega, 1] (Stark's own suggestion, p. 227), theta = omega for the principal class c_0, so eps(c_0) = 2 eta(2 omega)^2/eta(omega)^2.
4. By Weber's definition f(tau) = e^{-pi i/24} eta((tau+1)/2)/eta(tau) (our recall, consistent with Stark's p. 218 sentence) at tau = sqrt -p, (tau+1)/2 = omega, and eta(2 omega - 1) = e^{-pi i/12} eta(2 omega), we get f(sqrt -p) = e^{pi i/24} eta(omega)/eta(2 omega). Hence 2/f(sqrt -p)^2 = e^{-pi i/12} eps(c_0). The absolute values agree, so log|.|^2 agrees.
5. Conjugates: Stark sets eps(c') = eps(J)^{sigma(p)} for p in c' (p. 226). Since chi-bar(a) = chi(a-bar) and the modulus (2) is conjugation-stable, L(s, chi) = L(s, chi-bar), so the rho vs rho-bar convention in our claim costs nothing.

Status of steps 1-5: algebra by this seat, unchecked by a second seat. The numeric match is already in k1/k2 outputs. These steps only show that the claim IS Stark's f = (2) case.

## Gap that is genuinely open (one sentence)

Stark 1980 already contains Claim 1 (the f = (2) eta-quotient Stark unit, which is 2/f(sqrt -p)^2 up to a root of unity when 2 splits), so the only open item here is Claim 2: writing a non-Stark Ramanujan theta-quotient unit as three Siegel/Robert units times a first-limit-formula Delta quotient. Stark 1980 has nothing on that.
