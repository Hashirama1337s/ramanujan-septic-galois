I'll referee the argument against the papers it cites, and check whether the all-n claim and the prior-art sentence survive the pages themselves.**In plain words.** The argument that \(\mathbb{Q}(z_n)/\mathbb{Q}\) is abelian only for \(n=1\) is a proof for every integer \(n\ge 1\). For \(n\ge 2\) the commutator is the class of \(\bar\nu/\nu\), that class is not \(\pm 1\) in \((\mathcal{O}/5\mathcal{O})^\times\), and a Hauptmodul takes different values on different \(\Gamma(5)\)-orbits in the upper half-plane. For \(n=1\) the same commutator lands in the unit group and dies, so the whole ray class field is abelian over \(\mathbb{Q}\). The degree supplement, as written, does not prove that only \(\pm I\) fixes the value. The closed real-root count is still a sketch. The sentence "abelian over \(\mathbb{Q}\) only for \(n=1\)" was not found in anything opened here.

**Choice of \(\nu\).** Holds for every \(n\ge 2\), including \(5\mid n\) and \(25\mid n\). KSY (5): if \(D\equiv 0\pmod{4}\), then \(\tau_{\mathcal{O}}=\sqrt{D}/2\). For \(D=-4n\) this is \(i\sqrt{n}\), and (6) gives \(b_{\mathcal{O}}=0\), \(c_{\mathcal{O}}=n\), so KSY's Cartan is the note's \(g(t,s)=[t,-ns;s,t]\). Proposition 5.1 states the isomorphism \((\mathcal{O}/N\mathcal{O})^\times\to W_{\mathcal{O},N}\) for a positive integer \(N\), with no coprimeness line in the proposition. Section 2 defines \(\mathcal{C}_N(\mathcal{O})\) through the maximal order at modulus \(\ell_{\mathcal{O}}N\). Take \(x=1\), or \(x=2\) when \(5\mid(1+n)\). Then \(N(x+\tau_{\mathcal{O}})=x^2+n\) is a unit mod 5 in every class, including \(n\equiv 0\). Stevenhagen was not opened, so the citation underneath Proposition 5.1 was not rechecked.

**Commutator.** Holds. KSY §4: for \(\alpha\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})/\{\pm I\}\), \(f^\alpha=f\circ\widetilde\alpha\); for \(\mathrm{diag}(1,d)\), coefficients move by \(\zeta_N\mapsto\zeta_N^d\). With that rule, \(\varepsilon=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1)\) satisfies \(\varepsilon g(t,s)\varepsilon=g(t,-s)\), the matrix of \(t-s\tau_{\mathcal{O}}\). So \(\delta=\varepsilon\gamma\varepsilon\gamma^{-1}\) corresponds to \(\bar\nu/\nu\) or to its inverse. Both have norm 1. Neither is \(\pm 1\) in \(\mathcal{O}/5\mathcal{O}\): \(\bar\nu-\nu=-2\tau_{\mathcal{O}}\) and \(\bar\nu+\nu=2x\in\{2,4\}\), and neither lies in \(5\mathcal{O}\). Lemma 7.3 supplies \(\mathrm{Gal}(K_{\mathcal{O},5}/\mathbb{Q})=\mathrm{Gal}(K_{\mathcal{O},5}/K)\rtimes\langle\mathfrak{c}\rangle\). An inverse in the reciprocity convention replaces \(\delta\) by \(\delta^{-1}\) and still excludes \(\pm 1\).

**Value moves.** Holds, including at points with extra automorphisms. Duke: "\(r(\tau)\) is analytic in \(\mathbb{H}\) and has no zeros there," and "\(j\) is a Hauptmodul for \(\Gamma(1)\) and \(r\) one for \(\Gamma(5)\)." He defines a Hauptmodul as a modular function with exactly one simple pole. One simple pole means degree 1, so one value in \(\mathbb{C}\) comes from one \(\Gamma(5)\)-orbit in \(\mathbb{H}\). KSY §4 then gives \(\sigma_\delta(z_n)=R(\widetilde\delta\,\tau_{\mathcal{O}})\). The note's own equation \(a^2+nc^2=1\) forces the stabiliser of \(i\sqrt{n}\) in \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})\) to be \(\{\pm I\}\) for every \(n\ge 2\). KSY Lemma 6.1 is the isotropy of a form \(Q\), not of the point. The point computation is the one the proof uses. Equality of values forces the lift into \(\pm\Gamma(5)\), hence \(\delta=\pm I\), which was excluded. So \(\sigma_\delta(z_n)\ne z_n\). If \(\mathbb{Q}(z_n)/\mathbb{Q}\) were abelian, restriction from the Galois group in Lemma 7.3 would kill that commutator.

**\(n=1\).** Holds. Remark 5.2(i): \(\mathcal{O}^\times=\langle\zeta_4\rangle\) if \(D=-4\), and \(\langle\zeta_2\rangle\) otherwise. Here \(5=(2+i)(2-i)\), so \((\mathcal{O}/5\mathcal{O})^\times\simeq\mathbb{F}_5^\times\times\mathbb{F}_5^\times\) has order 16 and the norm onto \((\mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z})^\times\) has kernel of order 4. The classes of \(\{\pm 1,\pm i\}\) are four distinct norm-one elements, so they are that kernel. Every commutator is trivial in \(W/T\). The semidirect product in Lemma 7.3 is direct, and a subfield of an abelian extension of \(\mathbb{Q}\) is abelian over \(\mathbb{Q}\). The cosine formula is a control. It is not needed.

**\(5\mid n\).** Holds for Theorem R by the same two exclusions. The order \(|(O/5O)^\times|=20\) is not used.

**Degree supplement.** Not proved as written. Part (a) says \(\mathrm{diag}(1,d)\) acts trivially on rational coefficients, hence \(R^\gamma=R\circ\widetilde{\gamma'}\). That is true of \(R\). It is false of \(R\circ\widetilde{\gamma'}\), whose coefficients need not be rational. KSY's rule \((f^\alpha)^\beta=f^{\alpha\beta}\) fixes which side may carry the diamond. The note names both sides and proves neither. Part (b) and the sentence "\(\mathrm{Stab}\) has order 2" in §6.4 both use part (a). The icosahedral identity itself is KSY (1). Generation is not an input to Theorem R. The note says so, and that part is right.

**§6.4.** The count of \(c\)-fixed points in \(W/\{\pm 1\}\) is an elementary matrix count: 4 if \(5\nmid n\), 2 if \(5\mid n\). Surjectivity onto \(\mathrm{Cl}(\mathcal{O})[2]\) is labeled SKETCH. That closed formula is not a theorem.

**Prior art.** Not found, in the opened texts, as a classification of \(\mathbb{Q}(R(i\sqrt{n}))/\mathbb{Q}\). Duke's Theorem 1 is the radical criterion: radicals over \(\mathbb{Q}\) if and only if \(j(\tau)\) is and (2.5) is reducible over \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_5,j(\tau))\). The thesis Lemma 129 says: "The class field \(H_5=H_{5,\mathcal{O}}\) is generated by \(R(\tau)\) over \(K\)." Cais–Conrad's abstract says the singular values "generate specific abelian extensions of imaginary quadratic fields," and that a real imaginary-axis value lies in a real radical tower only if every odd prime factor of its degree is a Fermat prime. That is a radical obstruction. It is not this list of \(n\). Cho–Koo was not opened.
